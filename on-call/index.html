Simplifying On-call Planning in an ITSM System

Project Overview

Role
Sole product designer
Scope
Full redesign of on-call planning and its integration into the company’s ITSM platform.
Key challenge
Move a mature planning workflow into ITSM without disrupting familiar team workflows.
Result
NSAT increased from 69% to 75%, while MAU grew from 2,987 to 4,500 after the rollout.

Context

Support teams used the ITSM platform for incident management, while on-call planning lived in a separate legacy product. Teams had to switch between systems to manage related workflows.

The goal was to bring planning into ITSM while keeping the experience familiar and easier to maintain.

Problem

The existing planning tool was widely used, but had accumulated complexity over time.

Moving it into ITSM created an opportunity to simplify the experience — while preserving the parts of the workflow that already worked well for teams.

Users

The main users were support engineers, incident coordinators, and support team leads.

They had different responsibilities and access levels, from creating schedules to checking coverage and managing changes.

My Role

I was the sole product designer on the project, working across research, interaction design, validation, and implementation.

I owned the redesign of on-call planning and its integration into the ITSM platform.

Design Challenges

  • Move an established workflow into ITSM without making familiar tasks harder.
  • Design for teams working across 11 time zones and different responsibilities.
  • Simplify the experience while preserving important functionality and flexibility.

Key Design Decisions

Multi Time Zone

Teams worked across 11 time zones, making time interpretation critical for planning.

The team decided to use Group Local Time as the single source of truth. My role was to make this context clear and consistently visible throughout the planning experience.

Seamless Migration

Research showed that teams were already comfortable with the existing calendar model.

Instead of introducing a new pattern such as a Gantt-style schedule, I kept the familiar planning model and focused on simplifying the experience around it.

Process

01 — Understand Existing Workflows

I talked to support teams and explored how schedules were created, managed, and checked.

This helped identify pain points while separating them from patterns that were already working well.

02 — Validate Concepts

I explored different ways of bringing planning into ITSM and iterated on the concepts with users and stakeholders.

The direction was refined through feedback before release.

03 — Ship Under a Hard Deadline

The project had roughly four months for research, design, validation, and development.

I worked directly with backend developers while the data structure was being built in parallel. Together, we resolved interaction and technical constraints and delivered the MVP on time.

Less critical customization was moved to a later phase.

Solution

The new planning experience became part of the existing ITSM platform.

  • Simplified schedule creation and editing
  • Removed outdated or rarely used functionality
  • Added customization for relevant use cases
  • Made schedules and team coverage easier to understand
  • Aligned the experience with the rest of the ITSM platform

Outcome

The redesigned experience was successfully adopted by support teams across the organization.

Future Improvements

As the product evolved, more functionality was added to the team header, making it increasingly dense.

In the next iteration, I would simplify the header and make the schedule easier to access without scrolling, especially on smaller laptop screens.
