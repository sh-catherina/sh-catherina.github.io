Launching a New Service in an Employee App

Project Overview

Role
Sole product designer
Scope
End-to-end UX for bringing service monitoring to the employee mobile app.
Key challenge
Give engineers access to critical service health data during urgent situations without disrupting the existing desktop workflow.
Result
530 users in the first month, around 13% of the app’s monthly active users.

Context

The internal monitoring tool tracked product availability and system health, but was available only on desktop.

During incidents or after hours, engineers needed a laptop and VPN just to check critical service health.

Problem

The goal was to make service health easier to check when engineers were away from their computers.

The challenge wasn’t to fit the desktop experience onto a smaller screen, but to understand what mobile was actually useful for.

Constraints

The web version remained the source of truth, so metric names and the underlying backend structure could not be changed for mobile.

My Approach

Together with an analyst, I looked at the main monitoring scenarios and identified two modes:

  • Normal monitoring: checking service health as part of everyday work.
  • Incident response: quickly understanding whether a service had degraded and what needed attention.

Based on this, I designed the mobile experience primarily around incident response rather than trying to replicate the full desktop workflow.

I created wireframes and detailed prototypes and validated the direction with stakeholders before development.

Key Design Trade-offs

Technical Terminology

The web version used technical metric names that were not always immediately clear.

Since the web experience remained the source of truth, I kept the same terminology on mobile and solved the usability issue through information hierarchy:

  • Simple service overview first
  • Detailed metrics one level deeper

Desktop Density

The desktop dashboard was designed for detailed monitoring, while the mobile scenario was focused on quick checks.

I didn't try to preserve the same information density on mobile. Instead, the mobile experience prioritized the information needed to understand service health at a glance.

Solution

The mobile experience provided:

  • Instant access to service health
  • A clear status overview for quick incident checks
  • Consistent metric interpretation across web and mobile
  • Less dependence on a computer during incidents

Future Improvements

I would explore more flexible home widget customization, allowing users to choose and order the services that matter most to them.

This could better support engineers responsible for many services while keeping the default experience simple for the main audience.
